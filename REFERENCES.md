# Academic References & Bibliography - Artificial Life Lab

This document maintains academic references to foundational literature in artificial life, agent-based modeling, evolutionary computation, evolutionary game theory, population ecology, and communication evolution.

---

## 1. Artificial Life & Complex Systems
1. **Langton, C. G.** (1989). *Artificial Life: The Proceedings of an Interdisciplinary Workshop on the Synthesis and Simulation of Living Systems*. Addison-Wesley.
2. **Ray, T. S.** (1991). An approach to the synthesis of life. *Artificial Life II*, 10, 371-408.
3. **Bedau, M. A.** (2003). Artificial life: organization and emergence of living systems. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 7(11), 505-512.
4. **Adami, C.** (1998). *Introduction to Artificial Life*. Springer Science & Business Media.

---

## 2. Evolutionary Computation & Quantitative Genetics
1. **Holland, J. H.** (1992). *Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems*. MIT Press.
2. **Goldberg, D. E.** (1989). *Genetic Algorithms in Search, Optimization, and Machine Learning*. Addison-Wesley.
3. **Falconer, D. S., & Mackay, T. F.** (1996). *Introduction to Quantitative Genetics*. Longman.

---

## 3. Evolutionary Game Theory & Evolution of Cooperation
1. **Axelrod, R.** (1984). *The Evolution of Cooperation*. Basic Books.
2. **Maynard Smith, J.** (1982). *Evolution and the Theory of Games*. Cambridge University Press.
3. **Nowak, M. A.** (2006). *Evolutionary Dynamics: Exploring the Equations of Life*. Harvard University Press.
4. **Nowak, M. A., & Highfield, R.** (2011). *SuperCooperators: Altruism, Evolution, and Why We Need Each Other to Succeed*. Free Press.

---

## 4. Population Dynamics & Theoretical Ecology
1. **Lotka, A. J.** (1925). *Elements of Physical Biology*. Williams & Wilkins.
2. **Volterra, V.** (1926). Fluctuations in the abundance of a species considered mathematically. *Nature*, 118, 558-560.
3. **Murray, J. D.** (2002). *Mathematical Biology: I. An Introduction*. Springer.

---

## 5. Evolution of Communication & Information Theory
1. **Skyrms, B.** (2010). *Signals: Evolution, Learning, and Information*. Oxford University Press.
2. **Shannon, C. E.** (1948). A mathematical theory of communication. *The Bell System Technical Journal*, 27(3), 379-423.
3. **Lachmann, M., & Bergstrom, C. T.** (1998). Signalling among relatives. II. Beyond the handicap principle. *Journal of Theoretical Biology*, 195(1), 109-129.
